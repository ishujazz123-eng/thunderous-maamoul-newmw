How the Instagram Reels Algorithm Works (and What You Can Actually Control)

Updated November 4, 2026

Every creator wants to know why one Reel gets thousands of views while the next one barely moves. Instagram does not publish its exact formula, and anyone who claims to know it is guessing. What Instagram has said publicly, and what creators have observed over time, gives us a reliable picture of the signals that matter.

Reels are shown to people who don't follow you

Unlike the main feed, Reels are built for discovery. Instagram tests your video with a small group of people first. If that group watches, reacts, and shares, the video is shown to a larger group. If they scroll away quickly, distribution slows down. This is why a new account can have a Reel go viral while an older account's Reel flops.

The signals that seem to matter most

  • Watch time and completion. Do people watch to the end, or even watch it twice? A short Reel that people replay often does better than a long one they abandon after three seconds.
  • Shares. Sending a Reel to a friend through direct messages is one of the strongest signs that a video is worth showing to others.
  • Saves. A save tells Instagram the video is useful enough to come back to, which is especially powerful for tips, recipes, and tutorials.
  • Likes and comments. These still count, but they are usually weaker than shares and saves.
  • Audio and topic match. Instagram looks at the audio and the content of the video to decide who might enjoy it.

What does not help

Instagram has said it deprioritizes Reels that carry visible watermarks from other apps, are low resolution, or are mostly recycled from elsewhere. Reels that are blurry, cropped badly, or that have a logo from another platform in the corner are less likely to be recommended. Original content, filmed vertically and cleanly, has the best chance.

Practical steps you can take

  1. Win the first two seconds. Open with the most interesting moment, not a slow introduction.
  2. Make it rewatchable. Loop the ending into the beginning, or pack in a detail people miss the first time.
  3. Give people a reason to share. Ask yourself: who would I send this to, and why?
  4. Use text and captions. Many people watch without sound, so on-screen text helps them stay.
  5. Post consistently. Each Reel is a fresh test. More attempts mean more chances to find what your audience wants.

Read your own data

The best source of truth is your own Insights. Open a Reel's insights and look at average watch time and the number of shares and saves. Compare your best three Reels with your worst three. The pattern you see there is more valuable than any rumor about the algorithm.

The bottom line

You cannot control the algorithm, but you can control what you give it: a strong opening, a clear idea, and a video people want to finish and share. Treat each Reel as an experiment, learn from the results, and adjust.

This guide is for general information. Instagram features and rules change, so check the app for the latest options.
