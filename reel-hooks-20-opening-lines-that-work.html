Reel Hooks: 20 Opening Ideas That Stop the Scroll

Updated November 4, 2026

The first two seconds of a Reel decide whether someone keeps watching or swipes away. A hook is the opening line, image, or action that earns those extra seconds. Here are twenty ways to start, grouped by style.

Curiosity hooks

  1. "I wish I knew this earlier."
  2. "Nobody talks about this part."
  3. "This took me years to figure out."
  4. "Wait for the end."
  5. "You are doing this wrong, and it is not your fault."

Problem hooks

  1. "Tired of [problem]? Try this."
  2. "Here is why your [thing] keeps failing."
  3. "Three mistakes that cost me time."
  4. "Stop doing this if you want [result]."
  5. "If [problem] sounds familiar, watch this."

Value hooks

  1. "Here is how to [result] in 30 seconds."
  2. "A free way to [result]."
  3. "Save this for later."
  4. "Five tools I use every day."
  5. "The simplest way to [result]."

Story and visual hooks

  1. Start with the surprising result, then show how you got there.
  2. Open with a close-up of an interesting action.
  3. Start in the middle of a story: "So there I was..."
  4. Use a bold text line on screen with movement behind it.
  5. Show a before-and-after in the first second.

Make the hook honest

A hook should promise something the video actually delivers. Misleading openings may get a click, but people leave quickly, and low watch time hurts the Reel's reach.

Match the hook to your video

  • For tutorials, lead with the result.
  • For stories, lead with the tension.
  • For tips, lead with the problem.

Say it and show it

Use text on screen as well as your voice, since many people watch without sound. Keep the text short, and place it in the center area of the screen.

Test and track

Try a different hook style each week and compare how many people watch past the first three seconds in your Insights. Keep what works and drop what does not.
