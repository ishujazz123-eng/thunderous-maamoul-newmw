Copyright Basics for Instagram Creators: Music, Photos and Reposting

Updated November 4, 2026

Most creators do not set out to break copyright rules. They post a song they like, share a photo they found, or repost a funny video. But copyright applies on social media too, and understanding the basics can save you from takedowns and account problems. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

What copyright means

Copyright gives creators control over their original work, including photos, videos, music, writing, and artwork. If you did not create it and do not have permission, you generally cannot copy, share, or profit from it.

Music in Reels

Instagram's music library has licenses for many tracks, mostly for personal accounts. Business accounts often have a more limited selection. If you upload a video with music that you have added yourself, Instagram may mute it or remove it. Use the in-app audio library or royalty-free music with clear licenses.

Photos and videos from other people

Finding an image online does not mean it is free to use. Even if you credit the creator, you may still need permission. The safest approach is to ask the owner first, and to keep their written reply.

Reposting content

  • Use Instagram's own sharing tools, such as sharing to Stories or the Collab feature, where the original creator stays visible.
  • Ask for permission before reposting someone's work to your feed.
  • Give clear credit, but remember credit does not replace permission.
  • Do not use someone's content to promote a product without a clear agreement.

Fair use is limited and unclear

Some countries have rules that allow limited use for things like commentary, criticism, or education. These rules are narrow, differ by country, and are decided case by case. Do not assume that adding a disclaimer such as "no copyright intended" protects you. It does not.

Safe sources of content

  • Your own photos, videos, and music
  • Content you have written permission to use
  • Stock images and music with licenses that fit your use
  • Works clearly released under open licenses, with the terms followed

Protecting your own work

If someone copies your content, you can report it to Instagram through its copyright reporting form. Keep original files and dates as evidence, and consider adding a subtle watermark to important work.

When in doubt

If you are unsure whether you can use something, assume you cannot until you have asked. Original work is safer, more valuable, and better for building a loyal audience.

This guide is for general information. Instagram features and rules change, so check the app for the latest options.
