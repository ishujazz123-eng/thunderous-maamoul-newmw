How to Write a Good Instagram Bio in Under 150 Characters

Updated November 4, 2026

Your bio is the first thing people read when they land on your profile. You have 150 characters to answer three questions: who are you, what do you post, and why should someone follow? Here is how to do it well.

The three-part formula

  1. Who you are or what you do. A role, topic, or niche.
  2. What people get. The benefit of following you.
  3. What to do next. A link, a product, or a simple invitation.

Examples

  • "Home cook | Easy weeknight dinners in 30 minutes | New recipe every Friday"
  • "Photographer in Jaipur | Street and travel stories | Prints available below"
  • "Learning to code in public | Daily tips for beginners | Free resources below"

Tips for the name field

Instagram's search looks at your name field. Add a keyword that describes what you do, such as "Anita | Home Cook". This helps the right people find you when they search.

Write clearly, not cleverly

A clever line can confuse. A clear one wins. Visitors decide in a couple of seconds whether to follow, so make sure they understand your account instantly.

Use line breaks and a few emojis

Short lines are easier to scan than a single long sentence. A small emoji can mark each line, but avoid filling the whole bio with them.

Add a link that matters

Use your link for one important thing: your website, a signup page, or a page that lists your latest work. Update it when your focus changes.

Use Highlights as part of your bio

Highlights sit right under your bio. Name them clearly, like "Start here", "Tips", or "Reviews", so new visitors can quickly see the best of what you do.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the bio empty.
  • Filling it with inside jokes only you understand.
  • Listing too many unrelated roles.
  • Forgetting to update it when your focus changes.

Test and refine

After you rewrite your bio, watch your profile visits and follower conversion in Insights for a couple of weeks. If visits are high but follows are low, your bio or recent posts may not match what people expect.

This guide is for general information. Instagram features and rules change, so check the app for the latest options.
